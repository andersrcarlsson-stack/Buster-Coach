Lessons

Levels 1 to 3 teach the three habits a beginner gains most from, one per level, each building on the one before:

Level The lesson The opponent You learn
1 · Lesson: free pieces take what's free now and then leaves a piece unprotected on purpose to look for what you can take
2 · Lesson: your loose pieces don't give anything away never misses a piece you leave hanging to check your pieces before you move
3 · Lesson: threats see what's coming makes simple, direct attacks, and takes the piece if you don't answer to ask "what does it want?" before every move

The lessons add up: level 2 also tells you when something is free, and level 3 gives all three nudges.

Between the lessons the opponent plays like a weak but sensible player, so a lesson stands out in a calm game. A lesson comes about every 6 to 10 of its moves.

The lesson card. Before your move a card names the habit, never the answer: "Something is free.", "Check your pieces.", "What does Buster want?". After your move it tells you how it went: "You took the free knight.", "The knight on e5 was free.", "You kept your bishop safe.", "You answered Buster's threat." Your saved game keeps these words.

Coaching during the lessons is Full and can't be changed: the menu shows Full coaching (lessons). Only the warnings wait for you (press T for the threat), so the answer stays yours to find. When you move the slider to level 4 or higher, your own coaching level comes back.

Lessons off (Coaching → Lessons at levels 1–3): levels 1 to 3 are plain easy games, about 800, 1000 and 1200, with the coaching you choose.
